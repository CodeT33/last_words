The music of progress drowns out everything else. Even the stars above have grown silent, their light smothered by smoke that smells faintly of ash and ambition.
They say the city runs on steam. They are wrong. It runs on fear — a constant, hissing pressure that seeps into every alley, every dream. The gears don’t merely turn; they hunger. The night down here has texture — oily, metallic, and warm with breath that isn’t quite human. You can almost hear it whisper, asking what you’re willing to trade for one more dawn. Each step downward is a surrender. The stairwell twists like a thought that should never have been followed. Above, the world fades into myth; below, the air thickens with oil and regret. You learn to measure time not by sunlight, but by the rhythm of unseen gears — slow, patient, eternal. The city waits for you, deep in its gut, where no truth can survive uncorroded. Beneath the city’s fractured domes, the air is alive — not with wind, but with the slow respiration of engines. Pistons sigh in the dark, and brass veins pulse with stolen fire. No one remembers when the machines began to dream, but their murmurs echo through the tunnels like prayers in reverse. Down here, invention is a religion, and the faithful are chained to their own creation.
